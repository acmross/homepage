CUES - From my midterm teaching demo - The visualizer on the left is mine, it's running live - A slicer makes four decisions you never see - Two rods: guess which snaps, then break them - AI writes the bad code so no student gets called out - Graded full marks in CSE 502 SAY IT This is from my midterm teaching demo this semester. I call the lesson "Your slicer is a compiler." On the left is the interactive visualizer I made. When you 3D print something, the slicer software makes a bunch of decisions for you. How thick the layers are. Where the walls go. Students step through each stage themselves and see what got decided for them. Then the fun part. I hand out two printed rods. Same part, just printed in two different directions. Everybody writes down which one will snap first. Then we break them. Most people guess wrong, and that's the point. Now they want to know why. I do the same thing with AI. I have the AI write bad code, and we pick it apart together. Nobody's embarrassed, because nobody in the room wrote it. (~55 seconds.
