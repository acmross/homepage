Speech-language pathologists have a gold-standard test for how a child talks, called language sample analysis. But it takes hours to do by hand, so it rarely gets done. SATE uses AI to do it. The picture shows the whole thing. You record with the SATE recorder, the AI processes it, and you get a report. And the clinician stays in the loop at every step. In our pilot study with 20 speech-language pathologists, it cut the task time by 55 percent and the transcription errors by 53 percent. My part is the people side. I do the human factors work and the end-user testing with clinicians. And this week I'm at the ASHA Convention, that's the American Speech-Language-Hearing Association, at our booth, putting SATE in front of the people who would use it. Here's what I learned. Clinicians never ask me how the AI works. They ask: which part of the recording gave me this score? If they can't ask that, they won't trust it. I want to be clear that SATE is my advisor's project, and I'm one part of a team. But that question is the one I'm taking with me. (~60 seconds)
