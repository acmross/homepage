CUES - Six community colleges; if one didn't have the class, I went to another - Stigma is changing; unconventional paths - Robotics Club VP: co-founded the student-run Makerspace, workshops, 2nd at RoboGames - Student government VP: cyber security awareness, trees, club carnival, $1 tacos, open gym, rest areas - Women in Engineering conference, asked a CEO, SE3D internship - Media director, David Cohen campaign, we won - All from asking SAY IT I went to six different community colleges. If one didn't have the course I needed, I went to another one. That's how I got from two art degrees into engineering. And I absolutely loved it. I think there's a stigma about community colleges, and it's changing, because they're amazing. You're surrounded by people with unconventional paths who just want to learn. And that's where I learned how to get the most out of school. I asked to be a leader, and I became the vice president of the robotics club. We co-founded a makerspace that students built and ran. That's me at the open house. We ran workshops and competitions, and we took second place at RoboGames. Then I became the vice president of the student body, where I advocated for students' needs and learned to advocate for my own. We promoted cyber security awareness, planted trees on campus, and hosted a carnival for the clubs. And the one with real, long-lasting impact on students' basic needs was the one-dollar tacos initiative. We also advocated for open gym access and rest areas on campus. I joined Women in Engineering, and at one of their conferences I asked a CEO for insight, and she offered me an internship at SE3D, which makes bioprinters for classrooms. And I became the media director for David Cohen's city council campaign, and we won. Which is pretty awesome. None of that was in a syllabus. It all came from asking. (~75 seconds)
