Amanda Ross · Faculty Candidate Talk ·

Addresses: Research goals and how they fit the institution's goals and resources. CUES - My own lab: assistive tech you can question - 1: sense everyday life, not twice a year - 2: every score shows its evidence - 3: design with the people who use it - Small and affordable: bench, printers, partners, undergrads - I've written grants before SAY IT So where am I going? If I had my own lab, it would build assistive technology you can ask questions. Three goals. One, sense everyday life. A clinic sees you twice a year. I want low-cost devices that show how someone's doing at home. Two, show the evidence. Every score should link back to what produced it, so a person can check it, or argue with it. Three, design with the people. The folks who'll use it help design it from day one. That's what I did with surgeons, clinicians, and caretakers. And it fits a department like this one. It needs a small workbench, a few 3D printers, clinic partners, and curious undergrads. No supercomputer. And I've written the grants to fund this kind of work before. (~50 seconds)
