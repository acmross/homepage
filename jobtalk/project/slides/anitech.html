CUES - AniTech: technologies that support animal well-being - Fulbright cold calls; Australia Zoo Wildlife Hospital asked for a tree - About 3 in 4 koalas die in rehab - Spoke with over 15 wildlife centers - 1 koalas need a perch, up to 20 hours a day asleep - 2 fallen branches: can't be cleaned, wrong perch sizes - 3 hygienic, adjustable, modular, attachments - 4 quick concept an undergraduate working under me helped design - Best Product Innovation, ZinnStarter - Attached to the problem, not the solution SAY IT I also started a startup called AniTech, which builds technologies that support animal well-being. And the K-Tree is where it started. I applied for a Fulbright, and I wanted to go to Australia. So I cold-called and emailed dozens of places there, asking what devices they needed for wildlife. People wanted to help. And the Australia Zoo Wildlife Hospital said: we need an artificial tree for rehabilitating koalas. Here's why. About three in four koalas die in the rehabilitation process. After speaking with over 15 Australian wildlife centers, it was clear there are issues with the structures they use. Picture one: a koala's livelihood depends on finding a secure perch. They can spend up to 20 hours a day sleeping. Picture two is rehab today. The climbing structures are fallen branches pulled from wherever a rehabilitator can find them. They can't be cleaned adequately, so disease spreads between koala patients. And look at the perch. The koala is holding on to it, but ideally it would sit into the perch to rest. Picture three is our idea: hygienic, adjustable, and modular, with attachments like a leaf feeder and a cooling system. And picture four is a quick concept that one of the undergraduates working under me helped design. It's simple for now. We won Best Product Innovation at the Silicon Valley Innovation Challenge, and got into the ZinnStarter accelerator. Which is pretty awesome. Someone once told me: be attached to the problem, not the solution. That's AniTech. (~70 seconds)
