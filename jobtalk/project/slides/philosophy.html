Amanda Ross · Faculty Candidate Talk ·

Addresses: Teaching philosophy and the learning theories behind it. CUES - First grad class story: "Am I an idiot?" - Whole class felt relief, we bonded - My job: a space to ask without pressure - Guess first / wrong is fine / build for someone real - Papert, TPACK, SAMR SAY IT So, my first class in grad school was really hard for me. I came from art. I sat there for a few classes thinking, am I an idiot? Am I supposed to know this already? So I raised my hand, and I literally asked that. And it seemed like the whole class had this sense of relief. Other people spoke up too. We all accepted that we didn't know, but we were willing to find out. And we learned so much better after that. That's what I want my classroom to be. A space to ask questions without pressure. So here's how I do it. You guess first, before we run anything. Being wrong is fine, because I grade your thinking, not your guess. And you build for a real person, and they decide if it works. Not me. The ideas behind it: Papert says we learn by building. TPACK says the tech, the teaching, and the content go together. And SAMR, which for me is one question: does this tool change the task, or just make it digital? (~60 seconds)
