It's a contract medical device manufacturing company, so they design, develop, and manufacture devices for other companies. I can't talk about certain specifics because of NDA, but here's the gist. I supported design verification testing, validation, and quality documentation, and I improved manufacturing efficiency through process optimization and time studies. I worked on a copper IUD for Sebela, where I focused on design for manufacturability, and it's now commercially available. I also designed a dedicated fixture for our technicians so builds were faster and more consistent. I worked on an external womb system, focusing on the valve and catheter components, with leak detection and pull-force testing. And I worked on Aortix by Procyrion, which is a percutaneous mechanical circulatory support device, or a fancy way of saying a pump, for advanced heart failure patients. I also trained our interns in quality practices and protocols. So when I teach device design, I'm teaching what it actually takes to get something made. (~55 seconds)
