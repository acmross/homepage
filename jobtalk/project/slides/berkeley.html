Addresses: Overview of past research projects (first research experience). CUES - Community college student, Berkeley Transfer-to-Excellence, Eisen Lab - Big picture: Huntington's, Parkinson's, Alzheimer's - Seven disordered transcription factors in fruit flies - "We weren't curing diseases, but helping others do that" - Masks were the hardest part, so I automated it with Fiji macros - Presented at SACNAS - Why I want undergrads in my lab SAY IT My first real research was as a community college student. I applied to a summer program at UC Berkeley called Transfer-to-Excellence, because applications are just a formal ask. And I worked in the Eisen Lab. So, big picture: Huntington's, Parkinson's, Alzheimer's. Those involve proteins that don't fold completely, and we know a lot less about them. I studied seven of them in fruit flies. They're transcription factors, which are proteins that act as switches to turn genes on or off. As my mentor put it then, we weren't curing diseases, but we were helping others do that by clarifying proteins. Picture one is me screening embryos. Picture two is what the confocal microscope sees: our proteins, tagged so they glow, inside each nucleus. I measured how each protein spreads out in there. The hardest part was creating masks to pick out each nucleus. It was tedious to do each image one by one, so I automated the process with macros in Fiji. Picture three is the poster. I presented it at SACNAS, the largest national conference for diversity in STEM. That summer, someone gave a community college student a real project in a real lab. I want to do that for my students.
